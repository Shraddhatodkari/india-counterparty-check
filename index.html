India Counterparty Check

Before a bank or an advisor works with a company, someone has to confirm who it legally is, who owns it, and whether it is on a sanctions list. This tool does those checks from official sources, uses a trained matching model and an AI news screen, and shows where every fact came from. A person still makes the decision.

How well the name matching works

I changed real list names on purpose and checked whether the tool still found them. I also ran clean Indian company names to count false alarms.

Trained match model ML

A logistic-regression model learns from seven features of each name pair, such as how many rare words match exactly. It was trained on labelled pairs and tested on pairs it never saw.

How it works

Entity resolutionText normalisation, IDF word weighting and Damerau-Levenshtein fuzzy matching, then identifier comparison against registry numbers.
Machine learning MLLogistic regression (scikit-learn) trained on labelled name pairs. Runs in the browser from its saved weights.
AI news screening AIWeb search per company, then an AI language model classifies each item by category, status and legal entity, with a link to the article.
Rules and routingSeven written rules decide who reviews a case. No model can approve or clear a company.
Data pipelinePython downloads OFAC, UN and GLEIF data. Node.js runs the screening and the tests.
HostingA static site on GitHub Pages. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

What it does not do yet

  • It screens the OFAC and UN lists only. EU, UK and Indian lists are not included.
  • Ownership comes from GLEIF, which reports accounting parents. It does not show the individual beneficial owners.
  • Court, insolvency (NCLT) and GST records are not connected. Those need licensed or logged-in access.
  • A name match is a lead for a reviewer to check. Only a matching identifier, such as a registry number, shows two records are the same entity.
  • News screening reads headlines and short snippets, not full articles, and has not been checked by a person. It can miss items or misread them.
  • The match model was trained on deliberately changed list names, not on real analyst decisions.
  • Reports are a snapshot from the date shown. Lists and registry records change.
Sources: OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list (US Treasury) · UN Security Council Consolidated List · GLEIF Global LEI Index, which carries each Indian company's CIN from the Ministry of Corporate Affairs.
